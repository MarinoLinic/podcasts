Baby Essentials

A practical checklist for the first weeks. Essentials first, optionals in amber.

Essential
Optional / Nice-to-have
🌙 Sleep
Crib
Bassinet useful for first weeks; fits in parents' room
A full-size crib is the long-term sleep space. For the first weeks, a bassinet is a handy addition: smaller, easier to position beside the bed for nighttime nursing.
Firm crib mattress
Soft surfaces increase SIDS risk
Firmness is a safety requirement, not a comfort preference. Soft mattresses, pillows, and loose bedding are all SIDS risk factors. Stick to a firm, flat surface with a fitted sheet only.
Waterproof mattress pad
Goes under the fitted sheet to protect the mattress from blowouts and leaks. You'll be glad you have it at 3am when you're already doing a sheet change.
2–4 fitted sheets
Blowouts and spit-up happen at night too. Having several sheets ready means you can do a quick swap and get back to sleep without hunting for clean laundry.
3 swaddles or sleep sacks
Baby will have opinions; don't over-stock one type
Swaddles mimic the womb and help suppress the startle reflex that wakes newborns. Sleep sacks work better once babies can break free of swaddles. Babies often reject particular styles, so start with a variety before buying in bulk.
Optional
Bassinet or Moses basket
Smaller than a crib and easier to fit in the parents' room for the first weeks. Some have a drop side for easier nighttime access without fully getting out of bed.
Baby monitor
Audio or video check on the baby without entering the room. More useful once baby moves to their own room. Less useful if you're all sleeping in the same space.
White noise machine
Masks household sounds like doors, conversations, or traffic that can startle a sleeping baby. Many parents find it extends sleep stretches noticeably. A phone app or smart speaker works just as well.
Night light
Dim enough not to fully wake the baby during night feeds or nappy changes, but bright enough to see what you're doing. Warm amber tones are less disruptive to sleep than white or blue light.
🍼 Feeding
Nursing bras & nursing pads
If breastfeeding
Nursing bras open at the front for easier access without removing the bra. Nursing pads (disposable or reusable) absorb leaks between feeds, as breasts often let down on both sides at once.
6–8 bottles and nipples
More bottles means fewer washes; get dishwasher-safe ones
Newborns feed every 2 to 3 hours, so bottles pile up fast. Having 6 to 8 means you can let them accumulate and run one dishwasher cycle at the end of the day rather than hand-washing after every single feed. Fewer interruptions overnight. Don't stock up on one nipple brand; some babies are picky about flow and shape.
Burp cloths
Newborns spit up frequently, sometimes after almost every feed.. Drape over your shoulder while burping and keep several within reach at all times. Cloth nappies double up well here.
Optional
Bottle sterilizer
Dishwasher works just as well
A dishwasher on a hot cycle sterilizes bottles just as effectively. A dedicated sterilizer is only worth it if you don't have a dishwasher or want extra reassurance; it is otherwise a common regret purchase.
Bottle warmer
Many babies accept milk at whatever temperature it comes out: room temp or straight from the fridge. Worth trying cold/room-temp first before buying, since a bowl of warm water works just as well if warming is needed.
Breast pump
Useful for building a milk supply, relieving engorgement, and enabling someone else to feed. Manual pumps are quiet and portable; electric pumps are faster for regular use. Often covered by health insurance. Worth checking before buying.
Milk storage bags
Flat, freezer-safe bags designed for breast milk. More space-efficient than bottles in the freezer. Label with date and volume; frozen milk keeps for around 6 months.
High chair
~6 months
Only needed when solids start, usually around 6 months. No rush to buy early; it's a bulky item and babies shouldn't sit in one unsupported before they're ready.
🔑 Diapering
Diapers (multiple sizes)
Stock a small amount of newborn size and more of size 1; many babies skip newborn entirely or outgrow it within weeks. Avoid buying huge quantities of any single size in advance.
Baby wipes
Used at every nappy change. Fragrance-free and alcohol-free formulas are gentler on newborn skin. Unscented water wipes are a safe choice for the first weeks when skin is most sensitive.
Diaper rash cream
A zinc oxide barrier cream (like Sudocrem or Desitin) prevents and treats nappy rash by protecting skin from moisture. Apply at the first sign of redness; easier to prevent than to clear up.
Changing mat or pad
A waterproof, wipe-clean surface for nappy changes. Doesn't need to be a dedicated changing unit. A mat on the floor works fine and eliminates any fall risk.
Optional
Portable changing pad
A foldable version that fits in the nappy bag. Useful for changing on the go without having to use surfaces of unknown cleanliness.
Nappy disposal system
A dedicated diaper pail with odour-sealing mechanism. Regular bins work fine if emptied daily; the main advantage of a dedicated system is not having to take out the bag after every change.
👕 Clothing
12+ sleepers / pyjamas per size
Footed + hand mitts built in; newborns outgrow quickly
Newborns can go through multiple outfits a night due to blowouts. Sleepers with built-in feet mean no separate socks, and fold-over hand mitts prevent self-scratching. Buy mostly 0–3m and 3–6m sizes; newborn size is outgrown very quickly.
2 outfits (onesies)
For when you want something a bit more put-together than a sleeper. Make sure tops are actually onesies that snap at the crotch; regular tops ride up constantly and aren't practical on newborns.
Optional
Socks
Mostly unneeded with footed sleepers
Mostly redundant if you're using footed sleepers, which keep feet covered and warm without the effort. Useful for warmer weather when sleepers without feet are more comfortable.
2–3 hats
Essential early; newborns lose heat fast
Newborns lose a disproportionate amount of body heat through their heads and can't regulate temperature well. Hats are especially important in the first few weeks and in cooler environments.
Mittens
Prevents babies from scratching their own faces with their sharp new nails. Many sleepers have fold-over cuffs that do the same job. Check before buying separate mittens.
Jackets / outdoor suits
Weather and season dependent. Note: bulky coats should not be worn in car seats; use a thin layer under the harness and a blanket over the top instead.
🛁 Bathing
Baby bathtub or bath support
Keeps a newborn secure and at a safe depth in the water. Full-size baths are too deep and slippery. A small baby tub or a reclining seat insert gives you control with both hands.
Baby soap / shampoo
Fragrance-free, tear-free
Newborn skin is delicate and absorbs more than adult skin. Fragrance-free, tear-free formulas minimise irritation. In the first few weeks, plain warm water is enough; soap isn't necessary at every bath.
2–3 soft towels / washcloths
Regular towels work fine; hooded baby towels are cute but unnecessary. The key is having enough to rotate through without doing laundry after every bath.
Bath water thermometer
Target 37 °C; kitchen thermometer works
Babies can't tell you the water is too hot, and their skin burns more easily than adults'. The target is around 37 °C (98 °F), slightly warm to the touch. An elbow dip is a rough check, but a thermometer removes the guesswork. A regular kitchen thermometer works perfectly.
🚗 Transportation
Car seat
Infant (detachable) or convertible; both fine from birth
Infant seats are rear-facing only, have a detachable base and carry handle, and are convenient for moving a sleeping baby. Convertible seats grow with the child (rear- then forward-facing) but stay in the car. Either works from birth; choose based on lifestyle.
Stroller
Check compatibility with your car seat if you want a travel system (seat clicks directly onto the pushchair frame). A flat recline is important for newborns who can't yet support their head.
Baby carrier or wrap
Keeps baby close and soothed while freeing both hands. Wraps (stretchy fabric) suit newborns well; structured carriers are easier to use as babies get heavier. Finding the right fit is often trial and error; many libraries and groups offer rental or demo days.
Optional
Second carrier
Different carriers suit different ages, activities, and body types. A stretchy wrap for a newborn and a structured carrier for a heavier older baby is a common combination for parents who carry a lot.
Stroller accessories
A rain cover is worth having if you walk in variable weather; most strollers don't include one. Snack trays, footmuffs, and sun shades add convenience depending on your climate and habits.
Car seat base
Stays permanently installed in the car; the infant seat clicks in and out in seconds. Avoids re-threading the seat belt every time. Very convenient for one car; less practical if you switch cars often.
🩺 Health
Thermometer (temporal/forehead)
A temporal (forehead) thermometer is fast, non-invasive, and works on a sleeping baby. Rectal thermometers are most accurate for newborns but require more care. Avoid ear thermometers for babies under 6 months; ear canals are too small for reliable readings.
Nasal aspirator
Frida NoseFrida style is more effective
Newborns are obligate nose breathers and can't blow their noses. A blocked nose makes feeding difficult. The NoseFrida (mouth-operated suction tube) is significantly more effective than bulb syringes; there is a filter, you don't actually suck snot.
Baby nail clippers
Newborn nails grow surprisingly fast and are sharp enough to scratch their own face. Clip after a bath when nails are softer, or while baby is asleep. Baby scissors with rounded tips work well too.
🏠 Home
Pacifiers
Reduces SIDS risk; don't stock one shape
Pacifiers are linked to reduced SIDS risk and soothe babies between feeds. Many babies refuse them or have strong shape preferences. Buy a couple of different types before committing to one. If breastfeeding, some guidelines suggest waiting until feeding is established (around 3–4 weeks).
Optional
Changing table / dresser
A dedicated surface at waist height makes changes easier on your back. A chest of drawers with a changing topper works just as well and is more versatile long-term. A mat on the floor is the safest option, with no fall risk.
Rocking chair or glider
You'll spend hours in this chair, for feeds, settling, and soothing. The gentle motion helps calm babies and keeps you comfortable during long nights. Worth investing in if you plan to use it regularly.
Nursing pillow
Wraps around the waist to bring baby to the right height for breastfeeding without straining your arms and back. Also useful later for propping a baby during tummy time or supporting them as they learn to sit.
Optional
Bouncer or baby swing
Soothes and occupies baby hands-free, which is genuinely useful. Bouncers are compact and portable; swings have more motion settings. Many babies have strong preferences; borrow or buy second-hand before committing to an expensive model.
Pack-and-play / travel crib
Portable and versatile; useful as a backup sleeping space, a safe play zone, or for overnight trips. Many include a bassinet insert for newborns that can be removed as baby grows.
Diaper bag + caddy
A diaper bag organises nappies, wipes, a change of clothes, and feeding supplies for outings. A caddy (open-top portable tray) lets you move your changing essentials from room to room without a fixed changing station.
Baby activity gym
A padded mat with hanging toys and a play arch. Used for tummy time and supervised back play; the hanging objects encourage babies to track, bat, and eventually grab. One of the few early toys that actually gets used regularly.
